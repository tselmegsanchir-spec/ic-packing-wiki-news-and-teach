Part 1 · Lesson: learn the words first

What you will be able to do. After about 4 minutes you can explain 7 terms that appear in the news below, in your own words. Everything in Part 1 is background teaching (my own explanation), not newsletter facts; it follows the wiki glossary and concept pages.

1Package and OSAT

In one sentence
A package is the protective case and wiring that connect a tiny silicon chip (a die) to a circuit board. An OSAT is a company that assembles and tests chips for others.
Picture it
The die is a tiny engine; the package is the engine block with all the pipes and plugs.
Why a designer cares
A design only works if it can be packaged: heat, power and signals all have to get in and out.
Seen in the news: Power and wiring are becoming design problems for AI chips Denser packages bring heat and radiation problems
Wiki page: wiki/concepts/advanced-packaging-capacity.md

2Chiplet

In one sentence
A chiplet is a small die that works together with other dies inside one package, instead of making one giant chip.
Picture it
Building a house from prefabricated rooms instead of casting it as one block of concrete.
Why a designer cares
Small dies are cheaper to make well and can mix technologies, but the links between dies become part of your design.
Seen in the news: Denser packages bring heat and radiation problems
Wiki page: wiki/concepts/chiplets-and-hybrid-bonding.md

3Pitch and hybrid bonding

In one sentence
Pitch is the distance between neighbouring connections; smaller pitch means more connections in the same area. Flip-chip joins dies with tiny solder balls, while hybrid bonding presses copper pads directly together, so the pitch can be much smaller.
Picture it
Solder balls are a few big plugs; hybrid bonding is a dense field of tiny pins.
Why a designer cares
Denser links give more bandwidth for less energy between stacked dies, so designers must plan them early.
Seen in the news: Denser packages bring heat and radiation problems
Wiki page: wiki/concepts/chiplets-and-hybrid-bonding.md

4HBM and the base die

In one sentence
HBM is a tower of stacked memory dies placed right next to the processor. The base die at the bottom of the tower manages the connection to the processor.
Picture it
A tall car park next to a shop: many cars (data) can move at once.
Why a designer cares
AI chips depend on HBM bandwidth, and a customised base die makes memory part of the chip design.
Seen in the news: Memory gets expensive, and new kinds of memory are proposed
Wiki page: wiki/concepts/hbm-and-memory-wall.md

5Memory wall

In one sentence
The memory wall means a processor can compute faster than memory can deliver data, so moving data, not calculating, becomes the limit.
Picture it
A chef who cooks in seconds but waits an hour for the ingredients.
Why a designer cares
Moving data costs more energy than computing on it, so architects try to move less data, for example with near-memory computing.
Seen in the news: Memory gets expensive, and new kinds of memory are proposed
Wiki page: wiki/concepts/hbm-and-memory-wall.md

6EDA, RTL and verification

In one sentence
EDA is the software used to design chips. RTL is code that describes what the hardware does; verification checks it works before manufacturing; 'shift left' means finding bugs earlier.
Picture it
RTL is the blueprint written as code; verification is the building inspector who checks the blueprint before anything is built.
Why a designer cares
A bug found after manufacturing is far more expensive, which is why AI agents are being tried on verification and exploration first.
Seen in the news: Three articles, one message: AI helps design chips, people decide
Wiki page: wiki/concepts/ai-in-chip-design.md

7Foundry and process node

In one sentence
A foundry manufactures chips that other companies design. A node name such as 18A or 2 nm marks a manufacturing generation; the number is a marketing label, not a literal size.
Picture it
The foundry is a print shop and the node is its printing technology; you bring the artwork, your design.
Why a designer cares
Your design must follow the foundry's rules for its node, and capacity at advanced nodes is limited.
Seen in the news: Intel Foundry: first outside customer, and a push on yield
Wiki page: wiki/concepts/foundry-capacity-and-nodes.md

Check yourself (5 questions)

1. What does hybrid bonding do differently from flip-chip?

  • A) Uses bigger solder balls
  • B) Joins copper pads directly
  • C) Removes the package
Show answer

B) Joins copper pads directly. Hybrid bonding presses copper pads together without solder, so connections can be much closer.

2. Why are chiplets used?

  • A) Smaller dies are easier to make well
  • B) They remove the need for packaging
  • C) They replace memory
Show answer

A) Smaller dies are easier to make well. Small dies are cheaper to make well, and different technologies can be combined.

3. What is the memory wall?

  • A) Memory is too expensive
  • B) Moving data limits performance
  • C) Chips run too hot
Show answer

B) Moving data limits performance. Processors compute faster than memory delivers data.

4. What is verification?

  • A) Manufacturing the chip
  • B) Checking the design works before manufacturing
  • C) Selling the chip
Show answer

B) Checking the design works before manufacturing. Finding a bug before manufacturing is far cheaper than after.

5. A '2 nm' node name is...

  • A) The exact transistor length
  • B) A manufacturing generation label
  • C) A package size
Show answer

B) A manufacturing generation label. Node names are marketing labels for a generation, not literal measurements.

Three articles, one message: AI helps design chips, people decide

What happened

New to this? RTL is the code that describes what a chip does, cycle by cycle. High-level synthesis (HLS) turns shorter, C-like code into RTL automatically. A derivative design is a new chip built by changing an older one. The articles say AI is not yet good at producing RTL that meets power and speed goals.
Lesson: EDA, RTL and verification
AI take · hypothesis, not a fact. The tools will keep improving, but the part nobody can hand over is judging whether a result is right. Learning RTL, HLS and verification basics keeps you useful next to AI.
IC designAI accelerators · power and interconnect

Power and wiring are becoming design problems for AI chips

What happened
  • AI accelerators need high current at low voltage, which moves power delivery to vertical designs with regulators near the processor. [SPN-1123-12, 2026-11-23] Read full: Semiconductor Engineering ↗
  • Arteris analysed 1,000 silicon design starts and finds rising system complexity and growing importance of interconnects. [SPN-1125-11, 2026-11-25] Read full: Semiconductor Engineering ↗
  • Photonic design must be judged at system level, because parts that each meet spec can behave differently once combined into an optical link. [SPN-1125-10, 2026-11-25] Read full: Semiconductor Engineering ↗
New to this? A chip needs power delivered at a low voltage but a very large current, and big currents lose energy along the way. Putting the voltage regulator close to the processor shortens that path. An interconnect is the wiring network that carries data between blocks inside a chip.
Lesson: Package and OSAT
AI take · hypothesis, not a fact. Designers can no longer treat power and wiring as something the package team handles later. Expect them to appear as constraints from the first architecture draft.

Denser packages bring heat and radiation problems

What happened
  • Amkor's S-SWIFT uses embedded-trace RDL to give die-to-die links more bandwidth for AI, HPC and data-center chips. [SPN-1124-03, 2026-11-24] Read full: SPN paper page ↗
  • Alpha-particle emission is a growing soft-error risk as package density rises. [SPN-1125-03, 2026-11-25] Read full: SPN paper page ↗
  • Diamond Foundry made the first 200mm single-crystal diamond wafer, meant to spread heat from AI chips. [SPN-1124-09, 2026-11-24] Read full: Digital Today ↗
  • Paper: EOTPR fine-pitch probing isolates die-to-die interconnect defects in chiplet devices. [SPN-1120-03, 2026-11-20] Read full: SPN paper page ↗
New to this? A chiplet design splits one big chip into several small dies in one package. RDL is the extra wiring layer that connects them. A soft error is a bit that flips because of stray radiation, such as an alpha particle, without any permanent damage. The denser the package, the harder it is to cool, to test and to protect.
Lesson: ChipletPitch and hybrid bondingPackage and OSAT
AI take · hypothesis, not a fact. Test access, cooling and error protection will probably need planning at the same time as the logic design, not after it.

Memory gets expensive, and new kinds of memory are proposed

What happened
  • Tesla cut the memory of its AI4.5 chip from 32GB to 24GB because memory costs are rising, moving to LPDDR5X. [SPN-1123-02, 2026-11-23] Read full: TrendForce ↗
  • Nagare Labs, backed by Pat Gelsinger, is developing Z2RAM, a vertically stacked memory that claims SRAM-like speed with higher density. [SPN-1123-06, 2026-11-23] Read full: IEEE Spectrum ↗
  • SoftBank explored a $1 trillion purchase of SK Hynix but dropped it over financial and regulatory hurdles. [SPN-1124-08, 2026-11-24] Read full: EE Times ↗
New to this? SRAM is very fast memory made from transistors, but it takes a lot of chip area. DRAM is denser but slower and sits outside the chip. LPDDR5X is a low-power kind of DRAM. The memory wall means processors can compute faster than memory can feed them.
Lesson: HBM and the base dieMemory wall
AI take · hypothesis, not a fact. When memory is the costly part, architects may be asked to do the same job with less of it. Memory size may become a design target, not a given.
